Bounds & the Archimedean property

An infimum of −∞

Following a sequence as far down as we please.

\[ \inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\frac{n^2+1}{2n-1}\right\}=-\infty. \]

Intuition

Consider the function

\[ f:\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\tfrac12\}\to\mathbb{R}, \qquad f(x)=\frac{x^2+1}{2x-1}. \]

For large positive \(x\), dividing numerator and denominator by \(x\) gives

\[ f(x)=\frac{x+\frac1x}{2-\frac1x}\approx\frac{x}{2}. \]

So, when \(|x|\) is large, \(f(x)\) behaves like \(\tfrac12x\). The same asymptotic comparison holds as \(x\to-\infty\). For negative \(x\), the line with slope \(\tfrac12\) lies below those with slopes \(\tfrac13\) and \(\tfrac14\):

\[ \tfrac12x < \tfrac13x < \tfrac14x \qquad (x<0). \]

Consequently, for sufficiently negative \(x\), \(f(x)\) also falls below the lines \(y=\tfrac13x\) and \(y=\tfrac14x\). This explains why, for any real \(r\), we can find a negative integer \(n_1\) such that

\[ f(n_1)<\tfrac13n_1<r. \]

Either comparison line, and many others, can be used to prove that the values are unbounded below.

The real-domain graph excludes \(x=\tfrac12\), where the denominator vanishes. Every integer is in the domain. The comparison \(f(x)\approx x/2\) describes the tails; the exact slant asymptote is \(y=x/2+1/4\).

Formal argument

We will show that, for every real \(r\), some integer \(n_1\) satisfies \(f(n_1)<r\).

Proof

For \(n\le0\), the denominator \(2n-1\) is negative. Compute:

\[ \begin{aligned} f(n)-\frac n3 &=\frac{(n^2+1)-\frac n3(2n-1)}{2n-1}\\[4pt] &=\frac{n^2+n+3}{3(2n-1)}. \end{aligned} \]

The numerator is positive for every real \(n\), since

\[ n^2+n+3=\left(n+\tfrac12\right)^2+\tfrac{11}{4}>0. \]

Thus

\[ \boxed{f(n)<\frac n3\quad\text{for all integers }n\le0.} \]

Now let \(r\in\mathbb{R}\). By the Archimedean property, there exists a negative integer \(n_1\) such that \(n_1/3<r\). Then

\[ f(n_1)<\frac{n_1}{3}<r. \]

So the sequence takes values smaller than any prescribed real number. Therefore \(\inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)=-\infty\).□
